Frequently asked questions

Ask us
What is Outpost Classic?

A programme where Red Flag designs, brands, launches and runs your short-stay property end to end. You pay a one-time programme fee, then 8% of booking revenue or ₹4,999 a month, whichever is higher.

I already own a property. What do I pay?

The programme fee, electricity and utilities. No rent, no deposit.

I don’t own a property. Can I still join?

Yes. We help you find and assess a high-demand property before you sign a lease. You pay the programme fee, the rent and deposit to your landlord, and electricity.

12What is the Earn-Back Promise?

If your Outpost’s net operating profit in its first 12 full months after going live is less than the programme fee you paid, we pay you the difference, within 30 days of a valid claim. No questions asked.

T&C*

Is my return guaranteed?

No. Earnings depend on occupancy, rates, season and location, and a property can earn less or more than its costs. The only guarantee is the Earn-Back Promise, exactly as written.

Why is your commission only 8%?

Our operations run on an AI system rather than a large payroll, so each extra property costs us little to run.

Can I start with less than the cost, if I have a valid issue?

Yes, with Setup Flex. Talk to our team to see how it works for you.

How long until my property is live?

Under 45 days from signing, if the property is ready to work on.

When and how am I paid?

Twice a month, on the 1st and 16th, with a statement showing every booking and deduction.

Can I still use my property?

Yes. Owner nights are agreed at signing and blocked in the calendar. Nights you block beyond that allowance are excluded from the Earn-Back Promise.

I live abroad. Can I join?

Yes. NRIs can join fully remotely for a property in India. Payouts go to your NRO account, and Indian tax is deducted at source as the law requires.

Do you operate in Dubai, London and New York?

We run Indian Outposts for partners living there now. Dubai Outposts open in 2027 under local holiday-home licensing; London and New York follow where local short-stay rules allow.
